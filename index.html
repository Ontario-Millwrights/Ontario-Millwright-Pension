How the estimates work

Initial hours: 950, 1,500, 1,750, 1,870, then 2,000 per year. The fitter balances available annual and cumulative statement targets, limits hours to 0–5,000, and weakly favours the initial history. A close match does not establish the true annual hours.

Rates originate in the supplied pension calculator workbook, 1995–2025. The workbook's approximate 1.06 pension multiplier is included for 2024 and 2025. These are fixed effective annual rates from the workbook, not verified dated rate schedules. Midyear allocation, transfers and adjustments may prevent an exact match.

Estimated earnings = hours × JM wage × 1.10. This does not model apprentice rates, overtime premiums or other earnings. Hours are pension-credit hours and may differ from clock hours.

05 Your pension comparison

Other Pension Benefit Scenarios · Click to view all 18 options ▾

Illustrative option estimates. Relative option factors are approximated from one retirement example. Actual factors can depend on member and spouse ages and the plan’s actuarial basis. These are not administrator quotations.

Baseline default: the displayed defined benefit pension is assumed to be the life pension with a 10-year guarantee. Change this if your statement uses another form. A guarantee protects payments after death; the member’s pension remains payable for life. Supplement eligibility is unconfirmed; it stops at death or in the month age 65 is reached.

How the illustrative spouse-age adjustment works

This is a sensitivity model, not the pension plan’s actuarial basis. It estimates the relative cost of survivor protection using monthly discounted survival-weighted payments. Assumptions: 3% effective annual discount rate; a unisex Gompertz mortality curve with force of mortality 1% at age 65, doubling every 9 years; independent lifetimes; no mortality improvement or indexing. These numerical assumptions are chosen for illustration and are not fitted or validated against the plan’s experience.

The existing example-based option ratios are treated as a reference with a spouse three years younger than the member. Entering another spouse birth year scales each joint-and-survivor ratio by the modelled change in its payment cost at the member’s estimated retirement age. The 5- and 10-year guarantees are included. Single-life options are unchanged; a joint option cannot exceed the single-life amount with the same guarantee. A three-year age gap preserves the existing ratios. This does not recover the plan’s actual factors or establish a statistical fit from one assessment.

Member and spouse ages are approximate whole-year ages in the projected retirement year. Clearing the spouse field restores the original example-based factors. Background: actuarial joint-life and survivor payments
